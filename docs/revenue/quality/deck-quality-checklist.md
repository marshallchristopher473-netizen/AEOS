# Deck Quality Checklist

The production standard for every deck that leaves this shop — the portfolio sample
first, then every paid project.

**Provenance:** the failure modes below were identified in an audit of the SPED 607
Chapters 9 & 10 presentation (updated 27 October). That audit is recorded in the project
planning notes and was not independently re-verified when this checklist was written.
The checklist stands on its own regardless: each item is a defect worth blocking on.

## How to use it

Run it twice. Once when the build is finished, once more the morning after, before you
send anything. The second pass catches what the first one was too close to see.

**Blocking items must all pass.** A deck with an unchecked blocking item does not ship —
not to a client, not to a prospect as a sample. An unfinished sample argues against you
more effectively than no sample at all.

---

## A. Completeness — BLOCKING

The audit's central finding: eight content slides carried headings and nothing else.
A heading is a promise the slide has not kept.

- [ ] Every content slide has body content, not just a heading.
- [ ] Every content slide has speaker notes written out — not placeholders, not "TODO",
      not a repeat of the slide text.
- [ ] The speaker notes would let someone other than you deliver the slide.
- [ ] No slide contains lorem ipsum, bracketed placeholders, or a note to self.
- [ ] The deck has a title slide and ends deliberately; it does not simply stop.
- [ ] Slide count is within the agreed range.

## B. Density and hierarchy — BLOCKING

The audit flagged one slide carrying dense textbook material with repeated source lines.
Dense slides are the most common reason a presentation reads as unfinished.

- [ ] No slide exceeds roughly 40 words of body text. Count the worst one.
- [ ] Each slide makes one point. If it makes two, it is two slides.
- [ ] The reader can tell what matters most on each slide within three seconds.
- [ ] No paragraph where a short list would do; no list of one item.
- [ ] Source lines appear once, in a consistent position — not repeated inside body text.
- [ ] Nothing is pasted verbatim from a textbook or source document without being
      rewritten for an audience listening to a speaker.

## C. Sources and claims — BLOCKING

- [ ] **Every source listed has been opened by you.** Not recalled, not assumed from the
      title, not produced by an AI tool and trusted. Opened.
- [ ] Each citation names the organization and is current enough to be defensible.
- [ ] No statistic appears without a source you have personally confirmed.
- [ ] No claim about outcomes, time saved, or effectiveness appears unless the source
      actually supports that specific claim.
- [ ] Where the deck offers practical advice rather than research findings, it is framed
      as practice, not as evidence.

A single unverifiable citation can end a sales conversation. This section is the one
most worth the second pass.

## D. Attribution — BLOCKING

- [ ] If the material originated as shared or group coursework, your specific
      contribution is stated accurately.
- [ ] Nothing implies solo authorship of collaborative work.
- [ ] Client material is identified as the client's; your contribution is the structure,
      design, and notes.
- [ ] No third party's slides, images, or layouts are reused without the right to do so.

## E. Audience fit

- [ ] The intended audience is named in the intake and the deck matches it.
- [ ] Jargon is defined the first time it appears, or removed.
- [ ] The deck states what the audience should do afterward, and that action is
      specific enough to act on.
- [ ] Reading level and tone match the room — parents, staff, and leadership are three
      different rooms.

## F. Visual consistency

- [ ] One font pairing throughout.
- [ ] One color set throughout.
- [ ] Headings sit in the same position on every slide.
- [ ] Body text is the same size across comparable slides.
- [ ] Alignment is deliberate; nothing is a few pixels off from its neighbor.
- [ ] Text is readable from the back of a room — body text no smaller than 18pt.
      Captions and footnotes may go to 16pt, but no smaller, and never for a point
      the audience actually needs.
- [ ] Sufficient contrast between text and background.

## G. Mechanics

- [ ] Spellcheck run.
- [ ] Names, organizations, and titles spelled correctly — check every proper noun by eye.
- [ ] No orphaned punctuation or broken line breaks from pasted text.
- [ ] File opens correctly in both PowerPoint and Google Slides.
- [ ] File name is professional and includes the client or topic and a date.
- [ ] The client receives an editable file, not only a PDF.

## H. Privacy — BLOCKING

- [ ] No student names, initials, photographs, or identifiable work.
- [ ] No IEP content, assessment records, or other confidential educational information.
- [ ] Any classroom example is clearly composite or illustrative, and labeled as such.
- [ ] No internal client information that was not meant for the audience.
