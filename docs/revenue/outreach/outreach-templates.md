# Outreach — Five Contacts

The goal is **five relevant contacts, one paid project.** Not a campaign. Five people
you can name, each contacted once with a real reason.

**Gate:** do not send any of these until the sample passes the quality checklist. The
whole message depends on the sample being good.

## Who to contact, in priority order

1. **Independent education consultants** — they present constantly, bill their own time
   at a rate that makes $650 look cheap, and decide alone.
2. **Tutoring operators** — parent-facing events, marketing needs, and a budget they
   control.
3. **Small publishers and cultural organizations** — launch events and program
   introductions; your Third World Press and Chicago State experience is a real
   credential here, not a stretch.
4. **Program directors at nonprofits** — funder presentations and staff trainings, with
   a real deadline attached.
5. **Anyone with an event on the calendar in the next eight weeks** — urgency beats fit.

A warm contact beats a better-qualified cold one. Start with people who already know
your name.

## Rules

- **Attach the sample every time.** The sample is the argument; the email is the
  envelope.
- **One specific sentence about them.** Not flattery — evidence you looked. If you
  cannot write one honestly, pick a different contact.
- **Name the price.** Hiding it wastes a round trip and filters out nobody useful.
- **Never promise a date you have not checked** against your actual week.
- **Log the time.** Every email, every reply, every call goes in the log as
  `acquisition`. Acquisition hours are the ones that quietly destroy an effective rate,
  which is exactly why they must be counted.

## Handling the common replies

| They say | You say |
| --- | --- |
| "What's your rate?" | $650 flat for 8–10 slides with speaker notes and one revision. Send the one-pager. |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | No, but I can do a shorter deck for the same process. Do not drop below $500 — see the decision rule in `../offer/scope-and-terms.md`. |
| "Can you write the content too?" | That is a different project. Quote it separately, or decline — unbounded writing is what kills the hourly rate. |
| "Send me more samples." | Send the one. One finished sample beats three unfinished ones. |
| "Not right now." | Thank them, ask if you can check back in three months, and log it. |
| "Can you do this for my district?" | Pause. District procurement is a long cycle and outside this experiment. Take the meeting, but keep selling the $650 project. |
