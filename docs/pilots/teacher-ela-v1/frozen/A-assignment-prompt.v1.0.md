# Assignment A — "The City That Plants for Bees" (v1.0)

Synthetic material written for the AEOS teacher ELA pilot v1.0. The passage is fictional: Marrowfield is not a real city and every number in it is invented. It is not drawn from any published test, curriculum, or student work.

Intended band: Grade 6–8 informational reading with a short constructed response.

## Passage

**The City That Plants for Bees**

¶1 Five years ago, the city of Marrowfield had a problem that few residents noticed. Volunteers who counted insects in the city's twelve parks were finding fewer bees each summer. In their first count, they recorded 410 bees. Three summers later, the same count found only 260.

¶2 Bees matter to a city more than many people think. As bees move from flower to flower to collect food, they carry pollen that many plants need to make fruit and seeds. Marrowfield's community gardens grow tomatoes, squash, and berries, and gardeners had begun to complain that their plants were producing less.

¶3 The city council decided to try a simple plan. Instead of mowing every patch of grass short, city workers left strips along roads and in parks to grow wild. Students from two middle schools planted native wildflowers in the strips. They chose plants that bloom at different times so that bees could find food from spring until fall.

¶4 Not everyone liked the change at first. Some residents said the uncut strips looked messy, and a few worried that more bees would mean more stings. The council answered by putting up small signs that explained the project and by keeping the strips away from playgrounds.

¶5 Two summers after the strips were planted, volunteers counted again and recorded 480 bees, more than in the first count. Gardeners reported larger harvests of squash and berries. The plan also saved the city money, because workers spent fewer hours mowing. Since then, other towns have asked Marrowfield how to start bee strips of their own.

## Constructed-response task

Was Marrowfield's plan a good idea? Write one or two paragraphs. State a clear claim, support it with at least two details from the passage, and explain how each detail supports your claim.
