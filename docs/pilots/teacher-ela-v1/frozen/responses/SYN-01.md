# SYN-01

Synthetic response written for the AEOS teacher ELA pilot v1.0. It is not student work.

Marrowfield's plan to let strips of grass grow wild was a good idea because it brought the bees back and helped the city's gardens. The clearest proof is the bee count. Volunteers found only 260 bees before the plan, but two summers after the strips were planted they counted 480. That shows the wildflower strips gave bees enough food to return, which was the whole point of the plan.

The plan also helped people, not just insects. Gardeners reported larger harvests of squash and berries. Since bees carry the pollen these plants need to make fruit, more bees meant more food in the community gardens. Finally, workers spent fewer hours mowing, so the city saved money. A plan that solves the problem and costs less is a good idea.
