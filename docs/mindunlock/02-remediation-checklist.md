# Gate 0a — the remediation checklist

**The single highest-leverage hour of work available.** Deleting the fabricated figures needs no
replacement research and removes essentially all of the document's falsifiable exposure.
Everything else in this folder is improvement on top of a document that is no longer dangerous.

Every string below was located in `MindUnlock_Academy.jsx` by search, and the line numbers were
verified against the file — they are not reported from memory.

**These claims have almost certainly spread.** Run the same search over every pitch deck, Notion
page, grant draft, email, and slide that drew from this document. The `.jsx` is where they were
written; it is not where they now live.

---

## How to use this

Work top to bottom. Each row is a literal find-and-replace. Where a row says **DELETE**, remove
the claim rather than softening it — a weakened version of a fabricated number is still a
fabricated number.

| Priority | What | Why it comes first |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Fabricated figures | No source exists. Pure liability, zero cost to remove. |
| **2** | The unlawful inducement | No pilot data can ever make it true. |

| **4** | Misreported figures | Real studies, wrong units. Correct rather than delete. |

## Priority 1 — fabricated figures: DELETE

No source contains these. None was located by any lens or verifier.

### 1.1 · line 41

> `38% of middle schoolers experience debilitating test anxiety (Zeidner, 1998)`

**Replace with:**

> `Test anxiety is reliably associated with lower academic performance, and the association is stronger for students already at academic risk (von der Embse, Jester, Roy & Post, 2018, Journal of Affective Disorders).`

### 1.2 · line 164

> `85% of test anxiety interventions validated only on white, middle-class, neurotypical populations (Zeidner, 1998; von der Embse et al., 2018).`

**Replace with:**

> `Test anxiety intervention research has historically underrepresented Black, neurodivergent and low-income adolescents. We have not quantified this and do not claim a figure.`

### 1.3 · line 165 — the clearest tell

> `73% of MCPS families lack both.`

**DELETE the sentence.** A 1997 national theory paper cannot contain a Montgomery County
statistic. Keep the Hoover-Dempsey & Sandler sentence before it, which is correctly attributed.

If the underlying belief matters, make it a hypothesis the pilot tests:

> `We believe many families lack both content knowledge and confidence in academic support strategies. This is our hypothesis, not a finding, and the pilot is designed to test it.`

### 1.4 · lines 168 and 466

> `Peter Gray (2013): Self-directed learning produces 40% greater intrinsic motivation.`
> `Agency in learning design produces 40% greater intrinsic motivation`

**DELETE both figures.** Holt, Gray and Freire are theory and advocacy; none reports an effect
size. The defensible replacement, and it is better evidenced:

> `Self-Determination Theory (Ryan & Deci) identifies autonomy, competence and relatedness as drivers of intrinsic motivation. For students with disabilities specifically, self-determination instruction (Shogren, Wehmeyer; the Self-Determined Learning Model of Instruction) is an evidence-based practice and is IDEA transition-aligned.`

### 1.5 · line 464

> `Identity-based negative stereotypes suppress performance 15–20%`

**DELETE the figure.** Stereotype threat carries serious publication-bias and replication
concerns, especially in children and field settings.

> `Belonging and utility-value interventions have produced real achievement effects in randomised trials, concentrated among lower-achieving students (Walton & Cohen, 2011; Hulleman & Harackiewicz, 2009). Effects are small on average and depend on timing and framing.`

### 1.6 · line 465 — the most sensitive one

> `Culturally affirming instruction raises Black student achievement (ES: 0.8)`

**DELETE the effect size.** It is attributed to three theorists, none of whom published a
meta-analysis. **The genuine evidence is stronger than the invented number:**

> `Dee & Penner (2017, American Educational Research Journal) found large gains in attendance and credits earned for at-risk students assigned to an ethnic studies course, using a regression-discontinuity design. Aronson & Laughter (2016, Review of Educational Research) synthesise the broader culturally relevant education literature.`

### 1.7 · lines 75, 338, 400 — the consumer-facing one

> `90% faster study with AND without AI` · `Study 90% Faster` · `90% faster study facilitation`

**DELETE entirely, including the module title.** An unsubstantiated quantified performance claim
made to parents is the clearest consumer-protection exposure in the document. Rename the module
to what it does — e.g. *"Study Smarter: retrieval, spacing and calibration."*

### 1.8 · line 483

> `Result: 60–90% speed increase with equal or greater comprehension.`

**DELETE the sentence.** Rayner, Schotter, Masson, Potter & Treiman (2016, *Psychological
Science in the Public Interest*) conclude there is a speed–accuracy tradeoff and that reading
rate is driven by language skill and vocabulary, not eye-movement training.

Keep the three-pass structure — it is SQ3R-family previewing and has modest support — and drop
every speed number and the branded attribution. See §3.3.

## Priority 2 — the unlawful inducement: DELETE TODAY

### 2.1 · lines 334 and 356

> `Paid peer tutoring eligibility ($15–20/hr)` · `MCPS paid peer tutoring eligibility`

**Maryland's minimum working age is 14, and work permits are not issued below it.** The target
cohort is grades 6–8 — roughly ages 11 to 14. For most of them this benefit **cannot lawfully be
delivered**, and unlike every efficacy claim in this document, no amount of pilot data can ever
make it true.

It also raises a question the document never asks: **if MindUnlock pays the tutors, MindUnlock is
the employer**, with permit, hour, wage and recordkeeping obligations in every household it
serves.

Remove the payment claim. A peer-teaching practicum is pedagogically sound on its own — the
protégé effect is real — and needs no employment promise attached.

## Priority 3 — confabulated methods

### 3.1 · "NASA BARS" — lines 137, 338, 404, 482 (plus ~30 uses of the bare term)

There is no NASA method called BARS. In the literature **BARS means Behaviorally Anchored Rating
Scales** (Smith & Kendall, 1963, *Journal of Applied Psychology* 47(2):149–155) — a
personnel-appraisal scale-construction technique with no connection to scheduling. NASA's actual
workload instrument is NASA-TLX.

The underlying feature — decomposing work into short scheduled blocks with sleep protected — is
worth building. It simply cannot borrow a federal agency's name.

**Rename throughout** (e.g. *Mission Blocks*), and replace the method description with:

> `A 15-minute time-blocking and task-decomposition scheduler. Time-blocking and task decomposition are standard executive-function supports (Dawson & Guare, Executive Skills in Children and Adolescents). The MindUnlock implementation is unvalidated and is being evaluated in our pilot. Not affiliated with or derived from NASA.`

**Also delete line 137's instruction** to "Synthesize NASA BARS + learning science literature" —
that literature does not exist, so the instruction can only produce fabrication.

> **One legitimate use survives.** BARS is genuinely well-matched to the *certification
> rubrics* at lines 345 and 430, which rate human performance. Cite Smith & Kendall (1963)
> there, and nowhere else.

### 3.2 · "Anki SM-18" — lines 75, 97, 226, 459, 531, 594, 679, 774

Two unrelated products welded together. **SM-18 is SuperMemo's proprietary algorithm** — a
commercial competitor — and **Anki has never implemented it.** Anki uses a modified SM-2 and also
ships FSRS, which is open source.

**Replace with:**

> `Spaced repetition using FSRS (Free Spaced Repetition Scheduler), an open-source algorithm available in Anki. We implement FSRS directly in our own scheduler. We do not use, licence, or claim compatibility with SuperMemo's algorithms.`

**Three traps to avoid when rewriting:**

- **Do not write "Anki's default scheduler."** An audit lens made exactly this error. Anki's
  maintainer opened issue #3616, *"Make FSRS the default?"*, in December 2024; it was still open
  in August 2026. Verify against `docs.ankiweb.net` before any outward claim.
- **"AnkiConnect sync" (line 679) cannot work as described.** It is a local add-on requiring the
  Anki desktop client running on the same machine — not a server API. Replace with
  `.apkg import/export`.
- **Anki is AGPL-3.0.** Vendoring its code into a closed-source commercial product triggers
  network-use copyleft.

### 3.3 · "Wade Cutler's Triple Reading Speed" — lines 167, 338, 483, 531, 774

No peer-reviewed trial of the method was located. The publisher's only support is a self-reported
claim that it was taught to over 10,000 people in the Southwest between the mid-1960s and early
1970s — that is attendance, not outcome.

Keep the three-pass structure under a generic name. Drop the branded attribution and every speed
number. **Make no claim about the author**, in either direction.

> The book is real and still in print: Wade E. Cutler, *Triple Your Reading Speed*
> (Simon & Schuster; © 1970, 1988, 1993; 4th ed. 2002). Do not describe it as 1980s.

## Priority 4 — misreported figures: CORRECT, don't delete

These trace to the cited study. The unit or scope is wrong. **This distinction matters:**
"you fabricated this" is a charge you would lose in a room if the number actually traces.

### 4.1 · line 461

> `Mixed-topic practice beats blocked practice for long-term retention by 43%`

That is a **43-percentage-point gap between conditions** in one experiment (≈63% interleaved vs
≈20% blocked on a one-week delayed test), restated as a 43% improvement.

> `Rohrer & Taylor (2007) found substantially higher accuracy on a one-week delayed test for mixed practice than for blocked practice. Report the condition means, the delay, and the sample — not a single multiplier.`

### 4.2 · line 460

> `Self-testing produces 50% greater retention than re-reading`

A fair *relative* restatement of one Roediger & Karpicke one-week contrast (≈61% vs ≈40%),
presented as a general law.

> `Roediger & Karpicke (2006) found better one-week retention after repeated testing than repeated study. The advantage appears on delayed tests; on immediate tests rereading can look better — which is why students reliably choose the worse strategy.`

### 4.3 · line 459

> `Distributed practice produces 2× retention over massed practice`

Cepeda et al. (2006) is a real meta-analysis, but its finding is the **interaction between
spacing gap and retention interval** — not a blanket multiplier.

> `Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer (2006) found distributed practice outperforms massed practice, with the optimal gap widening as the retention interval lengthens. The practical consequence: a scheduler needs the test date as an input.`

## Priority 5 — debunked constructs: REMOVE FROM THE INSTRUMENT

### 5.1 · line 272 — VARK

> `5.1 VARK modality preference (Visual / Auditory / Kinesthetic / Reading-Writing)`

Learning-styles matching has no adequate evidence base (Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork, 2008,
*Psychological Science in the Public Interest*). **VARK is also a licensed, trademarked
questionnaire that forbids app embedding outright.** Both reasons are independently sufficient.

### 5.2 · line 271 — the worse one

> `4.8 Preferred cultural learning style (call-response, storytelling, movement)`

This attaches a disproven cognitive trait **to ethnicity**. Dressed as affirmation, it has the
same structure as the deficit theories it means to replace — and a journalist or parent will read
it that way, correctly.

**Replace both items with one question the child answers for themselves**, with no ethnic or
style label attached and no inference drawn from ancestry:

> `How would you like this explained?` — asked per lesson, answered by the student, changeable any time.

That keeps the agency the document genuinely cares about and removes the essentialism.

### 5.3 · schema — `student_profiles (… ancestry[])`

Storing a child's ancestry as a field that configures the product turns heritage into a
processing attribute. A content library a student can browse needs no stored ethnicity at all.
Make it optional, child-controlled, deletable in one tap, and firewalled from anything
pedagogical.

## Priority 6 — credential and compliance language

### 6.1 · "PhD-Level" — lines 34, 159, 258, 330, 341, 426, 454, 837

Delete every instance. It is not a property a 40-hour course for a twelve-year-old can have, and
it buys nothing — the quality of the programme is the claim.

### 6.2 · "CEU-eligible" — lines 99, 394, 419, 725

Delete until a **named** CEU-granting authority has approved in writing. Then name it.

### 6.3 · line 440 — named universities

> `Target partners: Howard University, Morgan State, UMCP`

Delete until a signed MOU exists. "Target" does not cure the implication, and it invites a demand
letter from each institution's general counsel.

### 6.4 · GED-to-PhD levels 4–8

Five of eight rungs are marked "Partner" with no partner. Sell only what you can deliver, and say
plainly that the certificate carries no academic transfer value. **One real articulation
agreement beats eight imaginary rungs.**

### 6.5 · lines 159 and 186 — "Systematic review"

> `Systematic review across 47 peer-reviewed sources, 24 courses, 38 apps`

A systematic review requires a registered protocol, a search strategy, inclusion criteria and a
flow diagram accounting for every record. **Call it a competitive scan** — which is a legitimate
and useful thing to have done.

### 6.6 · lines 117 and 638 — wrong jurisdiction

> `72-hour breach notification protocol`

That is the **GDPR Article 33** clock. FERPA imposes no breach-notification duty at all.
Maryland's Personal Information Protection Act gives **45 days** from discovery, with the
Attorney General notified in advance.

### 6.7 · Spielberger TAI — lines 264, 265, 398, 595

Remove as a product feature and as a certification competency. Two independent reasons:

1. The external licence is **research-only and not for individual feedback** — exactly what the
   product would do.
2. **It is normed for high school and up.** A twelve-year-old is outside the instrument
   *regardless of licensing* — which settles it without any negotiation.

### 6.8 · "crisis detection" and the `< 500ms` SLO

Remove the detection *claim* and the latency number. Replace with always-visible, unconditional
crisis resources that are not triggered by anything. Never publish a latency figure for a safety
function.

> The Maryland Consumer Protection Act reaches this kind of marketing claim without needing the
> unsettled negligent-undertaking tort theory — a much shorter path to the same liability.
