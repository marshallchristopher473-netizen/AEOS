## MCPS employee conflict of interest (Maryland Public Ethics Law, COMAR 19A.05, MCPS Policy BBB / Regulation GCA-RA)

### Bottom line

Read this with one environmental caveat up front: every primary-source host I tried was blocked in this session — mgaleg.maryland.gov, ethics.maryland.gov, regs.maryland.gov, law.justia.com, law.cornell.edu, and both montgomeryschoolsmd.org and ww2.montgomeryschoolsmd.org. WebFetch and curl both failed (DNS/proxy 403). So I could not read a single statute, regulation, or policy with my own eyes. Everything below rests on search-engine returns and page titles. Nothing here is a substitute for opening the four documents named.

What survives: the audit's institutional map is right, and its most load-bearing citation is now stale. MCPS Regulation GCA-RA (Employee Conflict of Interest), Board Policy BBB (Ethics), the Board of Education Ethics Panel, an Ethics Officer, and Form 460-50 as the outside-employment pre-approval all exist as designated. COMAR 19A.05.02.04 exists and does cover outside employment impairing impartiality plus disqualification from matters of direct economic impact. §5-506 exists and says substantially what the audit claims.

What breaks: §5-816 is the wrong cite as of 1 October 2025. SB 109 (2025), Ch. 299 renumbered §§5-816–5-820 to §§5-817–5-821; the conflict-of-interest provision is now §5-817, and §5-816 is a new annual certification duty. Also, Title 5 Subtitle 5 does not bind him directly — it reaches him through the Board's own adopted rules.

Operationally: the risk is a status prohibition (employment or financial interest in an entity contracting with the school system), which recusal does not cure. Do not pitch MCPS until the Ethics Panel says so in writing.

> The audit cites §5-816 (local boards of education) for the proposition that a local board adopts its own conflict-of-interest regulations and that county ethics provisions apply otherwise.

**What was actually checked.** Searched for the current content of GP §5-816 and for the sections around it. **Finding.** The substance is right; the citation is stale and is now wrong for current law. Senate Bill 109 (2025), enacted as Chapter 299, approved 6 May 2025 and effective 1 October 2025, renumbered §§5-816 through 5-820 of the General Provisions Article to §§5-817 through 5-821, and inserted a new §5-816 imposing an annual certification duty. The Justia 2025 page for §5-816 is now titled 'School Board's Certification to Ethics Commission' — on or before 1 October each year, each school board certifies to the Ethics Commission that it complies with the requirements of that Part for its members and, where applicable, its officials and employees. The 2023 edition's table of contents listed §5-816 as 'Conflicts of Interest' and §5-817 as 'Financial Disclosure'; the 10/1/2025 edition lists §5-817 as 'Conflicts of Interest.' The Commission's own Form 29 refers to §§5-817 and 5-818. So as of today, 30 October 2026, the proposition the audit attached to §5-816 lives at §5-817. The proposition itself is accurate: a school board may adopt conflict rules for school system officials and employees and must adopt them for its own members; staff rules must be similar to Subtitle 5 with permitted local modification, board-member rules must equal or exceed Subtitle 5; and unless the board adopts and maintains staff rules, the county's provisions enacted under §5-808 apply to that school system's officials and employees. Two further corrections of practical consequence. First, the §5-808 county fallback is a dead letter for him: MCPS has adopted its own rules (BBB and GCA-RA), so Montgomery County Code Chapter 19A and the County Ethics Commission are not his forum. If the audit told him to look to the County Ethics Commission, that is a wrong turn. Second, HB 1297 (2025) would have tightened local conflict provisions to equal or exceed State law; I could not confirm whether it was enacted, and he should not assume either way.

**Safe wording.**

> Under the Maryland Public Ethics Law as renumbered by Ch. 299 (SB 109, 2025) effective 1 October 2025, the provision letting a local board of education adopt its own conflict-of-interest regulations — with the county's §5-808 provisions applying to school system officials and employees if the board does not — is now General Provisions §5-817, not §5-816. I could not verify the verbatim current text, so treat the quoted wording as reported, not read.

#### **CONFIRMED — NARROWED**

> Selling to one's own school system is either a hard prohibition or something disclosure and recusal can cure; the audit should be precise about which parts are absolute and which are procedural.

**What was actually checked.** Searched for the prohibition language in MCPS Policy BBB and Regulation GCA-RA and for any exception or waiver. Could not open BBB or GCA-RA (MCPS hosts DNS-blocked). Evidence from search returns on https://www.montgomeryschoolsmd.org/ethics/ (which summarises BBB's prohibited activities), the GCA-RA detail page, and the COMAR 19A.05.02.04 review criteria described above.

**Finding.** The honest answer is that this splits into three different kinds of rule, and conflating them is where founders get hurt. (1) STATUS PROHIBITION — likely absolute, not curable by recusal. The MCPS ethics page lists among prohibited activities being employed by MCPS and a company that is subject to the authority of the school system, or that has negotiated or is negotiating a contract with the school system. This mirrors the State analog restricting employment with, or a financial interest in, an entity under an agency's authority or contracting with it. The violation here is the relationship itself, not any decision he participates in. Stepping out of the room does not cure it, because there is nothing to step out of — he is already in the prohibited posture the moment his company is negotiating with MCPS while he is an MCPS employee. I could NOT confirm whether BBB extends this to ownership/financial interest as well as employment, and I could NOT find any exception, waiver, or exemption provision in BBB. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence — but he must not assume a waiver exists. (2) PARTICIPATION PROHIBITION — procedural, curable by disqualification. COMAR 19A.05.02.04 requires disqualification from any school system matter with a direct economic impact on him, distinct from the impact on the public generally; the narrow exceptions are institutional (no one else can act; quorum). This is the part recusal addresses. (3) CONDUCT RULE — curable by abstention. The §5-506-equivalent prestige provision: not using his MCPS position, title, relationships, or access to advance the company, including the contract-influence prong. He can comply by simply not doing it, but note GCA-RA is reported to treat exploiting vendor relationships for personal benefit as a conflict, and his MCPS-earned credibility is exactly what a founder is tempted to spend. Plus (4) a separate procedural duty: Form 460-50 pre-approval, and financial disclosure (reported deadline 30 April, and within 60 days of leaving the position) if he holds a designated position — an undisclosed interest is its own violation independent of the underlying conflict. PRACTICAL BOTTOM LINE: the audit is right to treat this as serious and would be wrong to describe it as a disclose-and-recuse problem. But I cannot tell him it is a categorical bar, because I could not read the operative sentence. The proportionate posture: selling to MCPS while an MCPS employee is presumptively prohibited and should be treated as a no until the Ethics Panel says otherwise in writing; selling to other Maryland districts is a materially different and far safer posture that the audit may not have distinguished.

**Safe wording.**

> Three distinct rules apply and they are not interchangeable. First, a status prohibition — being employed by MCPS while also employed by, and likely while holding a financial interest in, a company subject to the school system's authority or negotiating a contract with it. Recusal does not cure this; the relationship itself is the violation. I have not confirmed whether BBB extends it to ownership, and I found no waiver or exception provision. Second, a participation prohibition — disqualification from any school system matter with a direct economic impact on me distinct from the public generally. This is procedural and recusal addresses it. Third, a conduct rule — not using my MCPS position, title, relationships or access to advance the company, including influencing a contract award. Separately, Form 460-50 pre-approval and financial disclosure are their own duties whose breach is independently actionable. I should treat selling to MCPS as presumptively prohibited until the Ethics Panel rules otherwise in writing, and should recognise that selling to other districts is a different and safer question.

**To settle it, open:** Policy BBB at https://ww2.montgomeryschoolsmd.org/departments/policy/pdf/bbb.pdf — find the prohibited-employment / financial-interest provision and read whether it reaches ownership and whether any exception, waiver or exemption exists. Then GCA-RA at https://ww2.montgomeryschoolsmd.org/departments/policy/pdf/gcara%20master.pdf for the enumerated conflicts. Then put the specific facts — I am an MCPS employee, I own company X, X would sell product Y to MCPS — in writing to the MCPS Ethics Officer, Board of Education Office, 240-740-3030, and request an Ethics Panel advisory opinion.

**What was actually checked.** Searched across montgomeryschoolsmd.org, ww2.montgomeryschoolsmd.org, BoardDocs and local news. **Finding.** All four exist as designated. GCA-RA is MCPS Regulation 'Employee Conflict of Interest'; it states its purpose as describing areas, in addition to those addressed in Maryland ethics law and Board Policy BBB, that constitute a conflict of interest, advising employees how to avoid them, and specifying what to do when a potential conflict arises. It was revised 15 August 2024 following Montgomery County Inspector General recommendations. BBB is Board of Education Policy 'Ethics,' a 26-page document most recently amended by Resolution No. 175-23, 20 April 2023, and reported to have been revised again in response to the new State law and new State Ethics Commission regulations. The Ethics Panel exists and is described in BBB as the advisory body responsible for interpreting the ethics policy and advising persons subject to it; the Board periodically advertises vacancies on it. An Ethics Officer sits in the Board of Education Office, reachable at 240-740-3030, and MCPS guidance directs staff with questions to contact their supervisor or the Ethics Officer first. BBB covers all MCPS employees, the superintendent, Board members, Board candidates, and lobbyists. Form 460-50 is the pre-approval instrument for outside employment, and its scope is broader than 'a second job': it reaches service as an officer, director, or board member of any non-MCPS entity, paid or volunteer — which captures being a founder-director of his own company. It is reported to ask the employee to attest that they reviewed GCA-RA and identified no potential conflict, and to require advance leave for any work done during the regular workday. Submitting it, and having a supervisor sign it, does not adjudicate a conflict and does not protect him if a conflict in fact exists. ## Clinical practice and instrument licensing

### Bottom line

The licensing half of this audit is right on the law and overstated on the conclusion. Md. Health Occ. §18-101(f)(1)(iii) and §18-401(a) say what the audit says they say — I pulled both from the General Assembly's own statute pages — and there is no software, ed-tech, or self-assessment exemption anywhere in §18-102 or §18-301(c).
